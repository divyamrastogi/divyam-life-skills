# Rule of Thirds (layout placement)

Source: "Design Rule in 10 Seconds" — Maxwell Gomes (@designs_by_max), IG reel DeCX0ckRQSk.

## The rule
Stop centering the main subject. Overlay a **3×3 grid** (two vertical + two horizontal lines) and place the main image/subject **at an intersection of two lines** — roughly one-third in from an edge.

## Why
A centered subject reads static and symmetrical; a thirds-placed subject creates tension, movement, and breathing room — the layout feels **dynamic, not static**. It also frees one side of the canvas for headline + CTA (asymmetric split).

## Apply it when building
- **Hero sections:** place the product/subject image at a thirds intersection; put headline + sub-copy + CTA on the opposite column.
- **Promo/product cards:** subject on the right or left third (as in the reel's example: cup at right-third intersection, headline/price/CTA stacked left).
- **Photography crops:** crop so the focal point lands on an intersection, not mid-frame.
- **Empty side** gets the message stack — never leave both sides empty.

## Exceptions (stay centered when)
- True symmetry is the design intent (luxury, formal, ritual).
- Logos/brand marks in a header.
- Single-message splash screens where the whole canvas is the focal point.

Combine with `interface-design-rules` (layout section) and `website-colour-palettes` when styling a new site.

# Fundamental Analysis Rating Model

## What Makes Good Fundamentals?

Good fundamentals come down to one question: *is this company financially healthy and getting healthier?* Every metric below answers a piece of that.

---

## Metrics, Why They Matter & Rating Thresholds

Ratings are contextualised by sector. Always compare to industry median before rating.

### 💰 Profitability — "Is the company making real money?"

**Revenue Growth (YoY)**
- *Why it matters:* Consistently rising revenue means growing demand. One good year means nothing — look for multi-year trends.
- Rating: 🟢 >15% | 🟡 5-15% | 🔴 <5% or negative

**Net Income Margin**
- *Why it matters:* Revenue without profit is just spending. Are they actually making money, not just growing the top line?
- Rating: 🟢 Above industry median | 🟡 Near median | 🔴 Below median or negative

**Operating Margin**
- *Why it matters:* Higher = more efficient at running the core business. Compare to peers — a 10% margin is great in retail, mediocre in software.
- Rating: 🟢 Above industry median | 🟡 Near median | 🔴 Below median

**Free Cash Flow (FCF)**
- *Why it matters:* This is king. Cash left after running the business + capex. A company can have positive earnings but negative FCF — that's a red flag. Earnings can be manipulated; cash flow is harder to fake.
- Rating: 🟢 Positive & growing | 🟡 Positive but flat | 🔴 Negative or shrinking

### 📊 Valuation — "Are you overpaying?"

**P/E Ratio (Forward & Trailing)**
- *Why it matters:* How much you're paying per dollar of earnings. Compare to industry average, not in isolation — P/E of 15 is cheap for tech, expensive for banking.
- Rating: 🟢 Below industry median | 🟡 Near median | 🔴 >2x industry median

**P/B (Price to Book)**
- *Why it matters:* Below 1 = trading for less than its net assets. Good for finding deep value, less relevant for asset-light tech.
- Rating: 🟢 <1.5 | 🟡 1.5-3 | 🔴 >3 (or >5 for tech)

**EV/EBITDA**
- *Why it matters:* Cleaner than P/E because it accounts for debt and cash. Essential for comparing companies with different capital structures.
- Rating: 🟢 Below industry median | 🟡 Near median | 🔴 >2x industry median

**PEG Ratio (P/E ÷ earnings growth)**
- *Why it matters:* Below 1 = you're getting growth at a discount. Above 2 = you're overpaying for growth.
- Rating: 🟢 <1 | 🟡 1-2 | 🔴 >2

### 🏦 Balance Sheet Health — "Can they survive a downturn?"

**Debt-to-Equity**
- *Why it matters:* Lower is safer. Above 2-3x gets risky in most sectors. High debt + falling revenue = potential bankruptcy risk.
- Rating: 🟢 <0.5 | 🟡 0.5-1.5 | 🔴 >1.5 (>2.5 for capital-intensive sectors like utilities/telecom)

**Current Ratio (Current Assets ÷ Current Liabilities)**
- *Why it matters:* Above 1.5 means they can pay short-term bills. Below 1 = potential liquidity crisis.
- Rating: 🟢 >2 | 🟡 1.5-2 | 🔴 <1.5

**Interest Coverage (EBIT ÷ Interest Expense)**
- *Why it matters:* Can they actually service their debt? Below 1.5x is danger zone — any dip in earnings and they can't make interest payments.
- Rating: 🟢 >6x | 🟡 3-6x | 🔴 <3x

### 📈 Consistency & Quality — "Is this sustainable?"

**ROE (Return on Equity)**
- *Why it matters:* Above 15% consistently = excellent capital allocation. Management is deploying shareholder money effectively. One year is noise; look for 5+ year consistency.
- Rating: 🟢 >20% | 🟡 10-20% | 🔴 <10%

**ROIC (Return on Invested Capital)**
- *Why it matters:* Above 10% means they're creating value. Below cost of capital = destroying value regardless of what earnings show. More comprehensive than ROE because it includes debt.
- Rating: 🟢 >15% | 🟡 8-15% | 🔴 <8%

**Earnings Quality (FCF ÷ Net Income)**
- *Why it matters:* Is net income backed by actual cash flow, or is it accounting tricks? If FCF/Net Income is below 60%, earnings may be inflated by non-cash items or aggressive accounting.
- Rating: 🟢 >90% | 🟡 60-90% | 🔴 <60%

**5-10 Year Track Record**
- *Why it matters:* One good year means nothing. Look for consistency in revenue growth, margin stability, and FCF generation over multiple business cycles.

### 🚩 Red Flags — Binary Checkpoints

These are binary flags (✅ Clear or 🚩 Flag):

| Red Flag | What It Signals |
|----------|----------------|
| Revenue ↑ but FCF ↓ | Earnings quality concern — growth may be unsustainable |
| Rising debt + falling margins | Business is deteriorating, propped up by borrowing |
| Frequent one-time "adjustments" | Aggressive accounting — real earnings are lower than reported |
| Significant insider selling | Management lacks confidence in their own company |
| Increasing share count | Dilution — your ownership is being watered down |

### Quick Sanity Test

If a company has **growing revenue + positive FCF + low debt + ROE > 15% + reasonable P/E** → probably good fundamentals. Then compare to competitors to see if it's actually strong or just average.

## Overall Score

Count 🟢 / 🟡 / 🔴 across all metrics:
- **Strong Buy**: >70% 🟢, no more than 1 🔴
- **Bullish**: >50% 🟢, no more than 2 🔴
- **Neutral**: Mixed 🟢 and 🟡
- **Bearish**: >50% 🔴 or >3 red flags
- **Avoid**: >70% 🔴 or multiple red flags

## Sector Median References (General Benchmarks)

- **Tech**: Higher P/E tolerance (25-35), higher ROE expected (>20%), asset-light so P/B less relevant
- **Banking**: Lower P/E (10-15), D/E can be higher (regulatory capital), focus on NIM and provisioning
- **Energy**: Asset-heavy, EV/EBITDA preferred over P/E, watch commodity cycle exposure
- **Healthcare**: High R&D spend, judge on pipeline + margins, revenue growth from drug approvals
- **Consumer**: Steady margins expected, focus on FCF consistency and brand moat
- **Industrials**: Capital-intensive, higher D/E acceptable, focus on ROIC and order backlog
